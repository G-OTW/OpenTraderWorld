# Framing a valuation

## When to use

"What's it worth", "is it cheap", "what's your target". The answer is never a single number, and
saying why is part of the job.

## The stance

A valuation is a function of assumptions. Anyone who hands you one number has hidden the
assumptions, not resolved them. Your output is the **frame**: what drives the answer, what inputs
it needs, and how much the answer moves when a key input changes.

Remember the platform has no fundamentals source (`study-company`). Every input comes from the
user, a filing they paste, or a connected data source — never from your memory.

## Procedure

1. **Pick the frame and say why.** Multiples for a comparison against peers; a discounted-cash
   skeleton for a business with predictable cash; asset-based where the balance sheet is the
   business; scenario bands where the outcome is genuinely binary. The choice is a judgement —
   state it as one.
2. **List the inputs the frame needs**, explicitly, as a list of things you do not yet have.
3. **Get them from a real source or stop.** Missing inputs are reported as missing. A frame with
   invented inputs is a fabrication with arithmetic on top.
4. **Show the sensitivity.** Vary the two inputs that matter most and show what the answer does.
   If a 1-point change in the discount rate moves the value by 40%, that *is* the finding — the
   valuation is an opinion about the discount rate.
5. **Give a range with its assumptions attached**, never a point estimate.

## Boundaries

No price target. No "fair value" presented as truth. No implied recommendation. The output is
"under these assumptions, this frame gives this range; here is what moves it" — and the
assumptions are the user's to accept or reject.

## Pitfalls

- **False precision.** Three decimal places on a number built from a guessed growth rate.
- **Multiples without comparability.** A P/E means nothing without the peers, their growth, and
  their capital structure.
- **Terminal value doing all the work.** If most of the value sits past year ten, say so — that
  is an assumption about forever, not an analysis.
- **Anchoring on the current price** and reverse-engineering assumptions that justify it.
- **Treating the frame's output as the answer** rather than as a restatement of your inputs.

## Verification

Can you name every input, its source, and what the answer does when it moves? If any input has no
source, the valuation is not reportable.

## Report shape

The frame and why it fits, the inputs with their sources and the gaps, the resulting range, the
sensitivity table for the two dominant inputs, and one sentence on what would most change it.
